**1.0 MVP OVERVIEW**

**1.1 Purpose of the MVP**

The Minimum Viable Product (MVP) of CarGo Kenya is the first functional version of the platform intended to validate the fundamental business concept before significant financial investment is made in advanced technology, integrations, infrastructure, and operational expansion.

The MVP will focus on solving one clearly defined problem:

**Can CarGo Kenya reliably connect vehicle owners requiring transportation with verified professional drivers and successfully coordinate vehicle deliveries through a trusted digital platform?**

The MVP will therefore prioritize the most important elements of the CarGo Kenya business model:

- Customer acquisition.
- Driver verification.
- Transportation requests.
- Driver matching.
- Pricing and quotations.
- Payment collection.
- Trip management.
- Vehicle inspection.
- Trip tracking.
- Delivery confirmation.
- Driver payment.
- Customer feedback.
- Platform commission.

The MVP will deliberately avoid unnecessary complexity that does not directly contribute to validating the core business proposition.

**1.2 MVP Philosophy**

The CarGo Kenya MVP will be developed according to the following principles:

**1.2.1 Trust First**

Because customers are entrusting valuable vehicles to transport professionals, every major workflow should contribute to trust and accountability.

**1.2.2 Simplicity**

The MVP should be simple enough for customers and drivers to understand without extensive training.

**1.2.3 Manual Where Necessary**

Not every process needs to be automated during the initial stage.

Where automation would significantly increase development complexity without providing proportional business value, CarGo Kenya may initially use controlled administrative processes.

For example, certain verification and dispute-resolution activities may initially require administrator involvement.

**1.2.4 Evidence-Based Development**

The MVP should generate real operational data that can be used to determine which features should be developed next.

**1.2.5 Scalability Without Premature Complexity**

The technical architecture should allow future expansion without requiring the first version to contain every future feature.

**1.3 Core MVP Business Hypothesis**

The MVP is designed to test the following business hypothesis:

**Vehicle owners and authorized customers are willing to use a trusted digital platform to find verified vehicle transport drivers, make payments through the platform, monitor transportation, and confirm delivery, while professional drivers are willing to use the platform in exchange for additional transportation opportunities and a verifiable professional reputation.**

The MVP will therefore validate both sides of the marketplace.

**Customer-side validation**

CarGo Kenya must determine whether customers:

- Trust verified drivers.
- Prefer a digital booking process.
- Are willing to pay through the platform.
- Value GPS tracking.
- Value digital vehicle inspections.
- Value documented trip records.
- Are willing to pay a platform service fee.

**Driver-side validation**

CarGo Kenya must determine whether drivers:

- Are willing to register.
- Are willing to complete verification.
- Are willing to accept platform jobs.
- Consider the platform fee reasonable.
- Value additional trip opportunities.
- Value their digital professional reputation.
- Continue using the platform after completing their first trips.

**1.4 MVP Target Market**

The initial MVP will focus primarily on vehicle transportation within Kenya.

The initial operational focus will be on vehicles originating from **Mombasa**, particularly vehicles that have recently been imported and cleared and require transportation to destinations such as:

- Nairobi.
- Kisumu.
- Nakuru.
- Eldoret.
- Meru.
- Other locations within Kenya.

The platform may eventually support transportation originating from other regions once sufficient drivers and customers are available.

**1.5 MVP User Categories**

The MVP will support three principal user categories.

**1.5.1 Customer**

A customer is an individual or authorized representative requiring a vehicle to be transported.

A customer may:

- Register an account.
- Create a transportation request.
- Provide vehicle information.
- Specify pickup and destination locations.
- Review estimated pricing.
- Review driver quotations.
- Select a driver.
- Make required payments.
- Monitor a trip.
- Review inspection information.
- Confirm delivery.
- Rate the driver.
- View previous trips.

**1.5.2 Driver**

A driver is a professional vehicle transport operator who uses CarGo Kenya to obtain transportation assignments.

A driver may:

- Register an account.
- Submit verification information.
- Create a professional profile.
- Receive eligible transportation requests.
- Submit quotations.
- Accept assignments.
- Complete pickup inspections.
- Record vehicle information.
- Record fuel information.
- Update trip milestones.
- Provide GPS location.
- Complete delivery inspections.
- Complete trips.
- Receive eligible payments.
- Build a professional reputation.

An unverified driver shall **not** be permitted to accept a transportation assignment.

**1.5.3 Administrator**

An administrator manages the CarGo Kenya platform and supports operational control.

Administrators may:

- Review registrations.
- Verify drivers.
- Approve or reject driver verification.
- Monitor active trips.
- Review payment records.
- Review inspection records.
- Manage users.
- Suspend accounts.
- Handle operational disputes.
- Review reported incidents.
- Monitor basic platform performance.
- Manage system settings.

**1.6 Customer Account Requirements**

Customers will be required to create an account before requesting a transportation service.

The MVP customer account should contain:

- Full name.
- Phone number.
- Email address where applicable.
- Password or approved authentication method.
- Account status.
- Registration date.
- Basic verification status.

The platform should maintain a distinction between:

**Account registration**

and

**Trip-specific information.**

This ensures that customers do not repeatedly enter their personal information for every transportation request.

**1.7 Driver Account Requirements**

Driver registration will be more comprehensive than customer registration because driver verification is central to the CarGo Kenya trust model.

The driver account will contain:

- Full name.
- Phone number.
- Email address where applicable.
- Profile photograph.
- National identification information.
- Driving licence information.
- Years of driving experience.
- Professional biography.
- Route experience.
- Verification documents.
- Verification status.
- Account status.
- Completed trips.
- Rating.
- Driver wallet information.

The driver account may initially remain inactive until the required verification process has been completed.

**1.8 Driver Verification Workflow**

The driver verification process will follow a controlled workflow.

**Step 1: Registration**

Driver creates an account.

**Step 2: Profile Completion**

Driver provides required professional information.

**Step 3: Document Submission**

Driver uploads required verification documents.

**Step 4: Administrative Review**

CarGo Kenya administrator reviews submitted information.

**Step 5: Verification Decision**

The application may be:

- Approved.
- Rejected.
- Returned for correction.
- Placed under review.

**Step 6: Account Activation**

Only an approved driver becomes eligible to accept transportation requests.

The driver will receive a **Verified Driver** status when approved.

**1.9 Driver Reputation**

CarGo Kenya will use completed platform activity to establish a professional reputation for drivers.

The customer-facing reputation system will include:

- Average rating.
- Number of completed trips.
- Route experience.
- Years of experience.
- Professional biography.
- Verified Driver status.

The platform should not allow drivers to artificially create completed trips or ratings.

Only completed trips conducted through CarGo Kenya should contribute toward the platform's verified performance history.

**1.10 Transportation Request**

A customer will create a transportation request by providing information about the vehicle and required journey.

The request will include:

**Vehicle Information**

- Vehicle make.
- Vehicle model.
- Vehicle year where available.
- Vehicle registration/chassis information where required.
- Current vehicle condition.
- Whether the vehicle is operational.
- Special handling requirements.

**Transportation Information**

- Pickup location.
- Destination.
- Preferred pickup date.
- Preferred delivery timeframe where applicable.
- Additional instructions.

**Supporting Information**

The customer may upload photographs or other relevant information where required.

**1.11 Pickup Location**

The pickup location must provide sufficient information for the driver to locate the vehicle.

The customer may provide:

- Location name.
- Physical address.
- GPS coordinates where available.
- Map location.
- Contact person.
- Additional instructions.

Where the vehicle originates from a clearing facility or port-related location, the relevant customer or authorized party must ensure that the vehicle is available for collection.

**1.12 Destination**

The customer must provide a clear destination before the driver accepts the transportation assignment.

The destination may include:

- City/town.
- Physical address.
- GPS location.
- Delivery contact.
- Additional delivery instructions.

If the destination changes after the trip begins, the change must follow the established **Destination Change Procedure**.

**1.13 Trip Pricing**

CarGo Kenya will not rely entirely on fixed route prices.

This is because transportation costs can vary based on:

- Vehicle type.
- Vehicle model.
- Vehicle condition.
- Route.
- Distance.
- Pickup location.
- Destination.
- Fuel requirements.
- Road conditions.
- Driver circumstances.
- Timing.

The MVP will therefore use a **quotation-based model supported by an estimated price**.

**1.14 Initial Price Estimation**

When a customer creates a request, the platform may generate an estimated transportation range.

The estimate may consider:

- Route distance.
- Historical route pricing where sufficient data exists.
- Vehicle category.
- Estimated fuel requirement.
- Basic operating assumptions.

The initial estimate is intended to provide guidance rather than function as a mandatory final price.

**1.15 Driver Quotation**

Eligible verified drivers may receive transportation requests that match their operational profile.

A driver may submit a quotation containing:

**Driver Transportation Fee**

and

**Estimated Fuel Budget**

The driver may also provide:

- Expected delivery time.
- Additional transportation considerations.
- Notes explaining unusual pricing.

The driver should not be required to maintain hundreds of fixed prices for every possible vehicle and destination combination.

This approach allows drivers to respond according to the actual circumstances of each trip.

**1.16 Smart Pricing - Future Enhancement**

The initial MVP will not require a sophisticated AI pricing engine.

However, the platform architecture should allow future development of a Smart Quote Engine.

The future engine may consider:

- Historical completed trips.
- Vehicle type.
- Vehicle model.
- Route.
- Distance.
- Fuel prices.
- Driver pricing history.
- Seasonal demand.
- Market demand.
- Previous customer bookings.

The system could eventually provide:

**Recommended Driver Fee + Estimated Fuel + CarGo Service Fee = Recommended Customer Price**

This feature will only be developed after sufficient real-world data has been collected.

**1.17 Fuel Management**

Fuel represents a separate cost component of vehicle transportation and will therefore be recorded separately from the driver's transportation fee.

The MVP will use a **Fuel Budget** rather than direct petrol-station integration.

The driver will estimate the fuel requirement for the journey.

The platform may provide an estimated fuel range to identify unusually high or low requests.

The approved fuel budget becomes part of the customer's total booking cost.

**1.18 Fuel Evidence**

Where fuel is purchased during a trip, the driver may be required to provide evidence of the transaction.

Evidence may include:

- Photograph of fuel receipt.
- Photograph of fuel transaction confirmation.
- Fuel station information.
- Amount paid.
- Date and time.
- Relevant trip reference.

Where technically practical, the platform should allow the driver to upload this evidence directly against the trip.

This provides an auditable record of fuel expenditure.

**1.19 Customer Booking Price**

The customer's final booking price will consist of:

**Driver Transportation Fee**

**Approved Fuel Budget**

**CarGo Kenya Service Fee**

\=

**Total Booking Price**

For example:

Driver Transportation Fee: **KSh 22,000**

Fuel Budget: **KSh 12,500**

CarGo Kenya Service Fee: **KSh 1,500**

Total Customer Price: **KSh 36,000**

The customer will receive a clear breakdown before confirming the booking.

**1.20 Platform Commission**

CarGo Kenya will generate revenue primarily through its service fee.

The service fee may initially be configured as:

- A fixed amount per trip; or
- A percentage of the driver transportation fee.

The platform should allow administrators to configure the applicable commission without requiring changes to the core application code.

The commission structure should be clearly communicated to drivers and reflected in the relevant transaction records.

**1.21 Booking Confirmation**

A transportation booking becomes confirmed when:

1. The customer selects the driver.
2. The applicable transportation price is accepted.
3. The customer completes the required initial payment.
4. The driver confirms availability where required.
5. The platform creates an official trip record.

The system will then assign a unique trip reference.

Example:

**CGK-2026-000125**

This reference will be used throughout the transportation process.

**1.22 Payment Structure**

CarGo Kenya shall require the customer to pay the full agreed trip cost through the platform before the transportation assignment becomes officially confirmed.

The total trip cost shall consist of:

· Driver transportation fee.

· Fuel budget.

· Applicable CarGo Kenya platform fee.

For example, where:

· Driver transportation fee = KSh 18,000.

· Fuel budget = KSh 6,000.

· CarGo Kenya platform fee = KSh 900.

The customer shall pay:

**Total Trip Cost = KSh 24,900**

The customer shall not be required to make additional payment to the driver for the agreed transportation fee unless an approved change to the trip results in an additional charge.

The customer's full payment shall initially be associated with the relevant Trip Wallet.

The driver shall not immediately receive the transportation fee in their Personal Wallet.

Instead, driver earnings shall be released according to verified trip milestones.

### First Driver Payment Release

After the driver has:

· Arrived at the pickup location.

· Completed the required vehicle inspection.

· Captured the required photographs.

· Recorded the odometer reading.

· Recorded the fuel level.

· Completed the vehicle handover.

· Completed any required fueling process.

The driver shall select:

**START TRIP**

The customer shall then receive a trip-start confirmation request.

Once the customer confirms the trip start, the system shall officially mark the transportation assignment as:

**TRIP ACTIVE**

At this point, 50% of the driver's transportation fee shall be released from the Trip Wallet to the driver's Personal Wallet.

### Final Driver Payment Release

The remaining 50% of the driver's transportation fee shall remain held within the Trip Wallet during transportation.

Following:

· Vehicle arrival at destination.

· Delivery inspection.

· Required delivery photographs.

· Final odometer and fuel recording where applicable.

· Vehicle handover.

· Customer confirmation.

· Driver confirmation.

The trip shall be marked as completed.

The remaining 50% of the driver's transportation fee shall then become eligible for release to the driver's Personal Wallet.

**1.23 Trip Wallet**

Each confirmed trip will have a dedicated Trip Wallet record.

The Trip Wallet will track:

- Customer payment.
- Fuel budget.
- Driver advance.
- Driver balance held for completion.
- CarGo commission.
- Refunds where applicable.
- Adjustments.
- Payment status.
- Release status.

The Trip Wallet is primarily an **accounting and transaction-management mechanism within the platform**.

It should not be treated as a general-purpose financial wallet for customers.

**1.24 Driver Wallet**

The Driver Personal Wallet shall represent funds that have become available to the driver following successful completion of the applicable trip payment-release conditions.

The wallet shall record:

· Released driver earnings.

· Available balance.

· Pending balance where applicable.

· Transaction history.

· Withdrawal history.

Driver funds shall only become available in the Personal Wallet after the corresponding release condition has been satisfied.

The Driver Personal Wallet shall remain separate from individual Trip Wallets.

A driver shall not be permitted to withdraw funds directly from an active Trip Wallet.

**1.25 Payment Release**

### Stage One - Trip Commencement

The first 50% of the driver's transportation fee shall become eligible for release when:

· Pickup inspection is completed.

· Required photographs are captured.

· Odometer is recorded.

· Fuel level is recorded.

· Vehicle handover is completed.

· Required fueling procedures are completed.

· Driver selects **START TRIP**.

· Customer confirms the trip commencement.

Once these conditions are satisfied, the system shall mark the trip as **TRIP ACTIVE** and release the first 50% of the driver's transportation fee to the driver's Personal Wallet.

### Stage Two - Trip Completion

The remaining 50% shall become eligible for release when:

· Vehicle reaches the destination.

· Delivery inspection is completed.

· Required delivery documentation is completed.

· Customer confirms successful delivery.

· Driver confirms completion.

The system shall then mark the trip as **COMPLETED** and release the remaining driver balance.

### Payment Protection

If a trip becomes subject to cancellation, dispute, failed pickup, or another exceptional event, funds associated with the affected trip shall remain protected until the applicable refund, settlement, or administrative decision has been completed.

**1.26 Trip Statuses**

Every trip will have a defined status.

The initial MVP statuses should include:

**REQUESTED**  
Customer has submitted a transportation request.

**QUOTING**  
Eligible drivers may submit quotations.

**DRIVER SELECTED**  
Customer has selected a driver.

**PAYMENT PENDING**  
Required payment has not yet been completed.

**BOOKED**  
Payment has been completed and the trip is confirmed.

**PICKUP PENDING**  
Vehicle has not yet undergone pickup inspection.

**PICKUP INSPECTION**  
The driver is at the pickup location and the required vehicle inspection, photographs, odometer reading, fuel recording, and vehicle handover procedures are being completed.

**TRIP START PENDING**  
The pickup procedures have been completed, but the trip has not yet officially started because the driver and customer have not completed the required trip-start confirmation.

**TRIP ACTIVE**  
The driver has selected **START TRIP** and the customer has confirmed the trip commencement. The transportation assignment is officially active and the first 50% of the driver's transportation fee becomes eligible for release to the driver's Personal Wallet.

**IN TRANSIT**  
The vehicle is actively being transported toward the destination.

**DELIVERY PENDING**  
The vehicle has reached or is approaching the destination and the delivery procedures are pending completion.

**DELIVERED**  
The vehicle has been delivered and the required delivery inspection and handover procedures have been completed.

**COMPLETED**  
The customer and driver have completed the required confirmation process and the trip has been formally closed. The remaining 50% of the driver's transportation fee becomes eligible for release to the driver's Personal Wallet.

**DISPUTED**  
A formal issue has been raised requiring administrative review.

**CANCELLED**  
The trip has been cancelled according to applicable cancellation procedures.

**1.27 Pickup Procedure**

The pickup stage is one of the most important points of the transportation process.

The driver must be present at the location where the vehicle is being collected. Before transportation officially begins, the driver and customer or authorized representative shall complete the pickup procedure.

The procedure will include:

1. Confirming the vehicle identity.
2. Confirming the pickup location.
3. Recording the odometer reading.
4. Recording the fuel level.
5. Photographing the vehicle from the required angles.
6. Recording any visible damage.
7. Recording relevant observations.
8. Confirming that the recorded vehicle condition accurately reflects the vehicle at pickup.
9. Obtaining confirmation from both the driver and customer or authorized representative.

After the pickup inspection and handover have been successfully completed, the trip shall move to **TRIP START PENDING**.

The driver shall then select **START TRIP**, after which the customer shall receive a trip-start confirmation request.

The trip shall only move to **TRIP ACTIVE** after the customer confirms that the transportation has officially commenced.

Once the trip becomes **TRIP ACTIVE**, the first 50% of the driver's transportation fee becomes eligible for release to the driver's Personal Wallet.

**1.28 Vehicle Condition Mismatch**

If the actual vehicle condition does not match the information originally provided in the transportation request, the discrepancy must be documented before transportation proceeds.

For example:

The customer declares:

"Vehicle has no visible damage."

During pickup, the driver identifies existing visible damage.

The driver should document the damage through:

- Photographs.
- Written observation.
- Timestamp.
- Location.
- Relevant inspection information.

The customer or authorized representative should be given an opportunity to acknowledge the discrepancy.

Where the difference is significant enough to affect transportation safety or pricing, the trip may be placed under administrative review before proceeding.

This prevents the driver from being held responsible for pre-existing damage.

**1.29 Vehicle Inspection Photographs**

The MVP should establish standardized inspection photography.

The exact number of photographs may be configurable, but the inspection should normally capture major vehicle areas such as:

- Front.
- Rear.
- Left side.
- Right side.
- Interior where relevant.
- Odometer.
- Fuel gauge.
- Existing visible damage.
- Other areas requiring special documentation.

The system should attach photographs to the specific inspection record rather than storing them as unrelated images.

**1.30 GPS and Trip Tracking**

The MVP will provide GPS-based trip visibility.

Tracking should begin once the trip officially enters the transportation stage.

The system may record:

- Current location.
- Timestamp.
- Trip status.
- Last known location.
- Movement history where technically feasible.

The MVP will not require advanced route optimization.

The purpose of tracking is primarily:

**To allow the customer and CarGo Kenya to know where the vehicle is during transportation.**

**1.31 Trip Milestones**

In addition to GPS tracking, the driver will update important transportation milestones.

Examples include:

- Pickup completed.
- Trip started.
- Fuel stop.
- Major route milestone.
- Temporary delay.
- Destination reached.
- Delivery inspection started.
- Delivery completed.

Milestones create an understandable journey record even when GPS data is temporarily unavailable.

**1.32 Network and GPS Failure**

The platform must account for temporary connectivity problems.

If the driver's device loses internet connectivity:

- The application should preserve relevant information locally where technically feasible.
- The last available GPS position should remain visible.
- New information should synchronize when connectivity returns.
- The system should record timestamps where possible.

The platform should not automatically mark a trip as failed merely because GPS connectivity is temporarily unavailable.

**1.33 Destination Changes**

A customer may request a destination change after transportation has started.

However, the change must:

1. Be submitted through the platform.
2. Be reviewed by the driver.
3. Be accepted by the driver.
4. Have any additional transportation costs calculated.
5. Be accepted by the customer.
6. Be permanently recorded against the trip.

The driver should not be forced to accept a destination change that materially changes the agreed transportation conditions.

**1.34 Trip Cancellation**

Once pickup has been digitally confirmed, neither the customer nor the driver may unilaterally cancel the trip.

If an exceptional circumstance occurs, the issue must be escalated through CarGo Kenya's support/dispute process.

Before pickup, cancellation rules may be different and should depend on the current booking status.

The system should record:

- Who initiated the cancellation.
- Date and time.
- Reason.
- Applicable financial consequences.
- Administrator decision where required.

**1.35 Delivery Procedure**

At the destination, the driver and receiving customer or authorized representative will complete the delivery process.

The process will include:

1. Confirming arrival.
2. Completing delivery inspection.
3. Recording vehicle photographs.
4. Recording odometer.
5. Recording fuel level where applicable.
6. Recording visible condition.
7. Recording relevant observations.
8. Obtaining customer confirmation.
9. Obtaining driver confirmation.

The trip then becomes eligible for completion processing.

**1.36 Trip Completion**

The trip is officially completed when:

- Vehicle delivery has occurred.
- Delivery inspection is completed.
- Customer confirms delivery.
- Driver confirms completion.

Once these conditions are met:

- Trip status changes to **COMPLETED**.
- Remaining eligible driver funds are released.
- Trip becomes part of the driver's completed-trip record.
- Customer becomes eligible to submit a rating and review.

**1.37 Customer Rating and Review**

After a completed trip, the customer may rate the driver.

The MVP will initially use a simple rating system, such as:

**1-5 stars**

The customer may optionally provide written feedback.

Ratings should only be associated with completed CarGo Kenya trips.

The driver's average rating should be calculated from eligible completed trips.

**1.38 Driver Earnings**

Driver earnings should be clearly distinguishable from the customer's total payment.

For example:

Customer pays:

**KSh 36,000**

The platform records:

- Driver Fee: KSh 22,000
- Fuel Budget: KSh 12,500
- CarGo Fee: KSh 1,500

The fuel budget is an operational trip amount and should not automatically be treated as driver income.

The driver's actual earnings should primarily represent the agreed transportation fee, subject to applicable adjustments.

**1.39 Customer Access to Trip Records**

Customers may access records relating to their own trips.

These may include:

- Booking information.
- Driver information.
- Payment information.
- Inspection records.
- Trip status.
- GPS/tracking information.
- Fuel records relevant to the trip.
- Delivery confirmation.
- Rating history.

**1.40 Driver Access to Trip Records**

Drivers may access records relating to trips assigned to them.

These may include:

- Customer information necessary for trip execution.
- Vehicle information.
- Pickup information.
- Destination.
- Payment information relevant to the driver.
- Inspection records.
- Fuel records.
- Trip milestones.
- Delivery information.

Drivers should not have unrestricted access to unrelated customer information.

**1.41 Administrator Access**

Administrators will have broader operational access.

Administrators may access:

- User accounts.
- Driver verification records.
- Active trips.
- Completed trips.
- Payment records.
- Inspection records.
- Disputes.
- Reports.
- Platform activity.

Administrative access must be controlled through appropriate permissions.

**1.42 Clearing Agent Participation**

Clearing agents are recognized as an important future and potentially early customer-acquisition channel.

However, the MVP should avoid creating unnecessary complexity around a separate clearing-agent ecosystem.

A clearing agent may participate through a customer account where appropriate.

If a clearing agent needs to arrange transportation on behalf of a customer, the transportation request should be created through the **clearing agent's own authorized account**, rather than pretending to be the vehicle owner.

This ensures that the platform maintains an accurate record of:

- Who created the request.
- Who authorized the transportation.
- Which vehicle is being transported.
- Who is the receiving customer.

A dedicated advanced Clearing Agent Portal can be developed later once actual usage demonstrates the need.

**1.43 Customer and Driver Contact Information**

CarGo Kenya should minimize unnecessary exchange of direct contact information before a booking is confirmed.

The purpose is not to prevent legitimate communication but to reduce the likelihood of users bypassing the platform.

Before booking:

Communication should primarily occur through controlled platform mechanisms.

After booking:

Appropriate contact information may be made available where necessary to facilitate practical vehicle collection and delivery.

This should be balanced against the practical realities of vehicle transportation.

**1.44 Platform Leakage Prevention**

CarGo Kenya's business model depends on customers completing transactions through the platform.

The platform will therefore provide sufficient value to discourage users from moving transactions outside the system.

Value provided includes:

- Driver verification.
- Trip documentation.
- GPS tracking.
- Digital inspections.
- Payment records.
- Delivery confirmation.
- Driver reputation.
- Customer support.
- Trip history.

The platform should not rely solely on hiding phone numbers to prevent leakage.

Its primary defense should be:

**Making the platform more valuable than bypassing it.**

**1.45 Dispute Handling**

The MVP will support basic dispute reporting.

A customer or driver may report issues relating to:

- Vehicle condition.
- Payment.
- Fuel.
- Destination changes.
- Delivery.
- Driver conduct.
- Customer conduct.
- Other transportation problems.

The MVP may initially use administrator-led dispute resolution rather than attempting to automate every possible dispute.

The system should record:

- Complaint.
- Parties involved.
- Evidence.
- Date and time.
- Administrator action.
- Resolution.
- Financial adjustment where applicable.

**1.46 MVP Notifications**

The system should notify users when important events occur.

Examples:

**Customer**

- Account created.
- Driver quotation received.
- Driver selected.
- Payment received.
- Trip confirmed.
- Pickup completed.
- Trip started.
- Significant trip update.
- Vehicle delivered.
- Trip completed.
- Driver payment released.
- Rating available.

**Driver**

- Verification approved/rejected.
- New trip request.
- Customer selected driver.
- Payment confirmed.
- Pickup scheduled.
- Trip started.
- Destination changed.
- Delivery confirmed.
- Earnings released.

**1.47 MVP Security Requirements**

Because the platform will process personal information, identity documents, vehicle information, payment records, and trip information, security must be considered from the beginning.

The MVP should include:

- Secure authentication.
- Password protection.
- Role-based access.
- Protected document storage.
- Secure API endpoints.
- Input validation.
- Authorization checks.
- Secure session/token management.
- Audit records for important actions.

Sensitive information must not be exposed to unauthorized users.

**1.48 MVP Data Records**

Every completed trip should produce a structured digital record containing:

- Trip reference.
- Customer.
- Driver.
- Vehicle.
- Pickup location.
- Destination.
- Price.
- Fuel budget.
- Platform fee.
- Payment status.
- Pickup inspection.
- Delivery inspection.
- GPS/trip records.
- Milestones.
- Completion confirmations.
- Rating.
- Relevant dispute information.

This trip record is one of the most valuable long-term assets of CarGo Kenya because it creates a verifiable history of the transportation process.

**1.49 MVP Administrative Controls**

Administrators should be able to configure basic operational settings without changing source code.

Potential configurable settings include:

- Platform commission.
- User status.
- Driver verification status.
- Trip statuses.
- Cancellation settings.
- Supported vehicle categories.
- Basic pricing parameters.
- Notification settings.

More advanced configuration can be introduced later.

**1.50 MVP Analytics**

The MVP will include only basic operational analytics.

Administrators should be able to view information such as:

- Registered customers.
- Registered drivers.
- Verified drivers.
- Active trips.
- Completed trips.
- Cancelled trips.
- Total transaction value.
- CarGo revenue.
- Average customer rating.
- Driver performance.
- Popular routes.

Advanced predictive analytics and AI-based business intelligence will remain future features.

**1.51 MVP Out-of-Scope Features**

The following will not be required for the first development release:

- AI-powered pricing.
- Automated route optimization.
- Automated insurance claims.
- Fuel station APIs.
- Automatic fuel payment to petrol stations.
- Cross-border transportation.
- Vehicle sales.
- Vehicle financing.
- Vehicle auction marketplace.
- Full corporate fleet management.
- Advanced clearing-agent portal.
- Advanced AI analytics.
- Predictive maintenance.
- Complex automated dispute resolution.
- Multiple financial products.
- International payments.

These features remain part of the future CarGo Kenya roadmap.

**1.52 MVP Success Criteria**

The MVP will be considered successful if it can demonstrate the complete transportation cycle:

**Customer Request → Driver Verification → Driver Quote → Booking → Payment → Pickup Inspection → Transportation → Tracking → Delivery Inspection → Completion → Driver Payment → Customer Rating**

The most important business validation questions will be:

1. Can CarGo Kenya attract customers?
2. Can CarGo Kenya attract trustworthy drivers?
3. Will drivers accept platform jobs?
4. Will customers trust verified drivers?
5. Will customers pay through CarGo Kenya?
6. Can CarGo Kenya successfully coordinate vehicle deliveries?
7. Will customers use the platform again?
8. Can the platform earn a sustainable commission from completed trips?

If the answer to these questions is consistently positive, CarGo Kenya will have evidence to justify expanding the platform.

**1.53 MVP Development Principle**

The MVP should not attempt to prove that CarGo Kenya can build a sophisticated logistics technology platform.

It should prove that:

**CarGo Kenya can successfully facilitate trusted vehicle transportation and create value for both customers and drivers.**

Technology should therefore support the business experiment rather than become the experiment itself.

**1.54 Transition to Future Development**

After the MVP has been tested with real users and sufficient operational data has been collected, CarGo Kenya may proceed to a second development phase.

The next phase may introduce:

- Smart Quote Engine.
- Automated fuel estimation.
- Advanced clearing-agent functionality.
- Corporate accounts.
- Insurance integrations.
- Fuel partnerships.
- Advanced analytics.
- Automated dispute management.
- Improved driver matching.
- Additional payment methods.
- Expanded geographical coverage.

The decision to introduce these features should be based on actual user behaviour, operational problems, revenue performance, and customer feedback rather than assumptions made before launch.

**1.55 Final MVP Definition**

For the purposes of development, the CarGo Kenya MVP can be summarized as follows:

**CarGo Kenya Version 1 is a digital marketplace that enables customers to request vehicle transportation, receive quotations from verified professional drivers, make payments through the platform, monitor transportation progress, document vehicle condition at pickup and delivery, confirm successful delivery, and provide driver feedback while CarGo Kenya earns a service fee from completed transportation transactions.**

This definition represents the **minimum viable product** and should serve as the foundation for the subsequent system design.

**2.0: SYSTEM MODULES AND FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS**

**2.1 Introduction**

This chapter translates the business requirements and MVP scope defined in Chapter 1 into functional system modules for the CarGo Kenya platform.

The purpose of this chapter is to define what the system should enable each user to do, what information should be captured, how the major processes should interact, and how the platform should enforce the business rules established for the MVP.

The system will be designed around three principal user categories:

- Customer.
- Driver.
- Administrator.

The modules described in this chapter are intended to provide the functionality required to support the complete CarGo Kenya transportation cycle:

**Customer Request → Driver Verification → Driver Quotation → Driver Selection → Payment → Booking → Pickup Inspection → Trip Start Confirmation → Transportation → GPS Tracking → Delivery Inspection → Completion → Driver Payment → Customer Rating**

The MVP will prioritize functionality that directly supports this transportation cycle. Processes that would introduce unnecessary technical complexity may initially be handled through controlled administrative procedures, as established in the MVP scope.

The User Authentication and Account Management Module will manage the creation, authentication, and maintenance of CarGo Kenya user accounts.

The module will support the three principal user categories:

- Customer.
- Driver.
- Administrator.

Each user will have an account associated with a specific role. The role will determine the functions and information that the user is permitted to access.

- Viewing quotations.

- Making payments.
- Monitoring trips.
- Viewing inspection records.
- Confirming delivery.
- Rating drivers.

**Registration → Profile Completion → Document Submission → Administrative Review → Verification Decision → Account Activation**

The driver shall then submit the required verification information and documents.

**REJECTED**

**CORRECTION REQUIRED**

The driver must provide additional or corrected information.

**UNDER REVIEW**

The application is currently being reviewed by an administrator.

**VERIFIED DRIVER**

status.

**2.7 Pricing and Booking Module**

The Pricing and Booking Module will determine and display the total amount that the customer is required to pay for the transportation assignment.

The total customer price shall consist of:

**Driver Transportation Fee + Fuel Budget + CarGo Kenya Service Fee**

For example:

| **Component**             | **Amount**     |
| ------------------------- | -------------- |
| Driver Transportation Fee | KSh 22,000     |
| Fuel Budget               | KSh 12,500     |
| CarGo Kenya Service Fee   | KSh 1,500      |
| **Total Customer Price**  | **KSh 36,000** |

The system shall clearly display the individual components and the total amount before the customer proceeds with payment.

**2.7.1 Driver Transportation Fee**

The Driver Transportation Fee represents the amount agreed for the driver's transportation service.

The amount may vary depending on factors such as:

- Route.
- Distance.
- Vehicle characteristics.
- Driver quotation.
- Transportation conditions.

The MVP shall allow the applicable transportation fee to be determined through the quotation process.

**2.7.2 Fuel Budget**

The Fuel Budget represents the amount allocated toward fuel required for the transportation assignment.

The fuel budget shall remain associated with the specific trip.

The fuel budget shall not automatically be treated as driver income.

**2.7.3 CarGo Kenya Service Fee**

The CarGo Kenya Service Fee represents the platform's revenue from facilitating the transportation transaction.

The applicable service fee shall be displayed to the customer before payment.

The fee may initially be configured as:

- A fixed amount per trip; or
- A percentage of the driver transportation fee.

The administrator should be able to configure the applicable fee without requiring changes to the core application code.

**2.7.4 Booking Confirmation**

A transportation booking shall become officially confirmed when:

1. The customer selects a driver.
2. The applicable transportation price is accepted.
3. The customer completes the required payment.
4. The driver confirms availability where required.
5. The system creates the official trip record.

The system shall then generate a unique trip reference.

Example:

**CGK-2026-000125**

The trip shall then move to:

**BOOKED**

**2.8 Payment and Trip Wallet Module**

The Payment and Trip Wallet Module will manage the financial transaction associated with each confirmed transportation assignment.

CarGo Kenya shall require the customer to pay the **full agreed trip cost through the platform before the transportation assignment becomes officially confirmed.**

The full customer payment shall initially be associated with the relevant Trip Wallet.

The driver shall not immediately receive the full transportation fee.

Instead, the driver's transportation fee shall be released according to the verified trip milestones established in Chapter 1.

**2.8.1 Trip Wallet**

Each confirmed trip shall have a dedicated Trip Wallet record.

The Trip Wallet shall track:

- Customer payment.
- Fuel budget.
- Driver advance.
- Driver balance held for completion.
- CarGo Kenya commission.
- Refunds where applicable.
- Adjustments.
- Payment status.
- Release status.

The Trip Wallet shall primarily function as an accounting and transaction-management mechanism within the platform.

It shall not be treated as a general-purpose customer wallet.

**2.8.2 Full Customer Payment**

The customer shall pay the full agreed trip cost before the trip becomes officially booked.

For example:

**Driver Transportation Fee = KSh 18,000**

**Fuel Budget = KSh 6,000**

**CarGo Kenya Service Fee = KSh 900**

Therefore:

**Total Trip Cost = KSh 24,900**

The complete KSh 24,900 shall be recorded against the relevant trip.

The driver's KSh 18,000 transportation fee shall remain controlled by the Trip Wallet until the applicable release conditions are satisfied.

**2.8.3 First Driver Payment Release**

The first 50% of the driver's transportation fee shall become eligible for release only after the required trip-start conditions have been completed.

These conditions include:

- Driver arrives at pickup location.
- Vehicle inspection is completed.
- Required photographs are captured.
- Odometer is recorded.
- Fuel level is recorded.
- Vehicle handover is completed.
- Required fueling process is completed.
- Driver selects **START TRIP**.
- Customer confirms trip commencement.

Once these conditions are satisfied:

**TRIP START PENDING → TRIP ACTIVE**

The first **50% of the driver's transportation fee** shall then become available for release from the Trip Wallet to the driver's Personal Wallet.

**2.8.4 Final Driver Payment Release**

The remaining 50% of the driver's transportation fee shall remain held in the Trip Wallet during transportation.

The remaining amount shall become eligible for release after:

- Vehicle reaches the destination.
- Delivery inspection is completed.
- Required delivery photographs are captured.
- Vehicle handover is completed.
- Customer confirms delivery.
- Driver confirms completion.
- Trip is marked **COMPLETED**.

The remaining 50% shall then be released to the driver's Personal Wallet.

**2.8.5 Payment Protection**

If a trip becomes subject to:

- Cancellation.
- Failed pickup.
- Dispute.
- Delivery problem.
- Another exceptional event.

The affected funds shall remain protected within the applicable transaction process until the issue has been resolved according to CarGo Kenya's applicable procedures.

**2.9 Driver Personal Wallet Module**

The Driver Personal Wallet will represent funds that have become available to the driver after satisfying the applicable payment-release conditions.

The Driver Personal Wallet shall remain separate from individual Trip Wallets.

The wallet shall contain:

- Available balance.
- Pending balance where applicable.
- Released driver earnings.
- Transaction history.
- Withdrawal history.

**2.9.1 Wallet Balance**

Only funds that have been released from a Trip Wallet shall become part of the driver's available wallet balance.

For example, if the driver's transportation fee is:

**KSh 18,000**

After the trip becomes **TRIP ACTIVE**, the first:

**KSh 9,000**

may become available.

The remaining:

**KSh 9,000**

will remain associated with the trip until the completion conditions have been satisfied.

**2.9.2 Withdrawal**

The driver shall only be permitted to withdraw funds that are available in the Personal Wallet.

A driver shall not be permitted to withdraw funds directly from an active Trip Wallet.

This separation provides additional protection against situations where a driver receives payment but subsequently fails to complete the transportation assignment.

**2.10 Pickup and Vehicle Inspection Module**

The Pickup and Vehicle Inspection Module will document the vehicle's condition before transportation officially begins.

The driver must be physically present at the location where the vehicle is being collected.

The customer or authorized representative shall participate in the pickup process where applicable.

**2.10.1 Pickup Procedure**

The pickup procedure shall include:

1. Confirming vehicle identity.
2. Confirming pickup location.
3. Recording odometer reading.
4. Recording fuel level.
5. Capturing required photographs.
6. Recording visible damage.
7. Recording relevant observations.
8. Confirming the recorded vehicle condition.
9. Obtaining confirmation from the driver.
10. Obtaining confirmation from the customer or authorized representative.

The inspection information shall be associated with the relevant trip.

**2.10.2 Pickup Inspection Status**

When the driver begins the inspection, the trip shall enter:

**PICKUP INSPECTION**

The driver shall complete all required inspection information before the system allows the process to continue.

After successful pickup inspection and handover:

**PICKUP INSPECTION → TRIP START PENDING**

The driver may then select:

**START TRIP**

The customer shall receive a trip-start confirmation request.

**2.11 Vehicle Condition and Evidence Module**

The Vehicle Condition and Evidence Module shall maintain a digital record of the vehicle's condition at pickup and delivery.

The purpose is to establish evidence that can help protect both customers and drivers.

The system shall allow the driver to record:

- Vehicle photographs.
- Odometer reading.
- Fuel level.
- Visible damage.
- Written observations.
- Date and time.
- Pickup location.
- Delivery location where applicable.

**2.11.1 Vehicle Condition Mismatch**

If the actual condition of the vehicle differs from the condition declared in the transportation request, the discrepancy shall be documented before transportation proceeds.

For example, if a customer declares that the vehicle has no visible damage but the driver identifies existing damage during inspection, the driver shall record the discrepancy using:

- Photographs.
- Written observations.
- Inspection information.
- Timestamp.
- Location.

The customer or authorized representative shall be given an opportunity to acknowledge the recorded condition.

Where the discrepancy is significant enough to affect safety, transportation requirements, or pricing, the trip may be placed under administrative review before proceeding.

This process helps prevent the driver from being held responsible for damage that existed before transportation began.

**2.12 Trip Start and Customer Confirmation Module**

The Trip Start and Customer Confirmation Module will control the point at which transportation officially begins.

This is an important part of the CarGo Kenya payment and accountability system.

The process shall follow:

**PICKUP INSPECTION**

↓

**TRIP START PENDING**

↓

Driver selects **START TRIP**

↓

Customer receives confirmation request

↓

Customer confirms trip commencement

↓

**TRIP ACTIVE**

Once the trip becomes **TRIP ACTIVE**, the first 50% of the driver's transportation fee becomes eligible for release.

The system shall therefore not release the first driver payment merely because the driver has been selected or because the customer has made payment.

The payment release shall depend on the completion of the defined physical pickup and trip-start conditions.

**2.13 GPS and Trip Tracking Module**

The GPS and Trip Tracking Module will provide visibility into the vehicle's transportation journey.

GPS tracking shall primarily begin once the trip officially enters:

**TRIP ACTIVE**

The system may record:

- Current driver location.
- Last known location.
- Timestamp.
- Trip status.
- Movement history where technically feasible.

The customer shall be able to view relevant trip progress through the platform.

The administrator shall also be able to monitor active trips where appropriate.

The MVP will not require advanced route optimization.

The primary purpose of GPS tracking is to provide visibility and accountability during transportation.

**2.14 Trip Milestone Module**

GPS tracking will be supported by manually recorded transportation milestones.

The driver may update milestones such as:

- Pickup completed.
- Trip started.
- Fuel stop.
- Major route milestone.
- Temporary delay.
- Destination reached.
- Delivery inspection started.
- Delivery completed.

Milestones shall be associated with the relevant trip and recorded with appropriate timestamps.

The purpose of milestones is to create a clear journey record even when GPS information is temporarily unavailable.

**2.15 Network and GPS Failure Module**

The MVP shall account for temporary network or GPS connectivity problems.

If the driver's device temporarily loses internet connectivity:

- The application should preserve relevant information locally where technically feasible.
- The last available GPS position should remain visible.
- New information should synchronize when connectivity returns.
- Relevant timestamps should be retained where possible.

A temporary GPS or internet failure shall not automatically cause the trip to be marked as failed.

The system should distinguish between:

**temporary connectivity failure**

and

**actual transportation failure.**

Where necessary, an administrator may review prolonged or unusual tracking interruptions.

**2.16 Destination Change Module**

A customer may request a destination change after transportation has started.

However, the change shall be processed through the CarGo Kenya platform.

The customer shall submit the requested change through the trip interface.

The system shall then:

1. Record the requested destination.
2. Notify the driver.
3. Allow the driver to review the request.
4. Allow the driver to accept or reject the change.
5. Calculate any applicable additional transportation cost.
6. Present the revised cost to the customer.
7. Obtain customer acceptance where an additional charge applies.
8. Record the approved change against the trip.

The driver shall not be forced to accept a destination change that materially changes the agreed transportation conditions.

**2.17 Delivery and Vehicle Handover Module**

**DELIVERED**

- Audit records for important actions.

**2.27.1 Role-Based Access**

Customers should only be able to access their own account and trip information.

Drivers should be able to access information necessary for trips assigned to them.

Administrators shall have broader operational access based on their assigned permissions.

Users should not be able to access unrelated customer, driver, payment or trip information.

**2.28 MVP Administrative Controls**

The system shall provide administrators with basic controls for managing platform operations without requiring changes to the application's source code.

Configurable settings may include:

- Platform commission.
- User status.
- Driver verification status.
- Supported vehicle categories.
- Basic pricing parameters.
- Cancellation settings.
- Notification settings.

The MVP should avoid introducing unnecessary configuration complexity.

Advanced configuration can be introduced in later development phases.

**2.29 MVP Analytics Module**

The MVP shall provide basic operational analytics.

Administrators should be able to view information such as:

- Registered customers.
- Registered drivers.
- Verified drivers.
- Active trips.
- Completed trips.
- Cancelled trips.
- Total transaction value.
- CarGo Kenya revenue.
- Average customer rating.
- Driver performance.
- Popular routes.

The MVP will not require advanced predictive analytics or artificial intelligence.

The purpose of the initial analytics functionality is to provide sufficient information for evaluating the performance of the business and determining what should be developed next.

**2.32 Chapter Summary**

Chapter 2 has defined the functional system modules required to implement the CarGo Kenya MVP.

The modules are directly derived from the business requirements established in Chapter 1 and are designed to support the complete transportation lifecycle.

The most important system relationship is the connection between:

**Transportation Request → Driver Selection → Full Payment → Trip Wallet → Pickup Inspection → Trip Start Confirmation → 50% Driver Payment Release → Transportation → Delivery → Completion → Remaining 50% Driver Payment Release → Driver Rating**

The MVP will therefore provide a controlled digital environment in which customers can request vehicle transportation, select verified professional drivers, make full payments through the platform, monitor transportation, document vehicle condition, confirm delivery, and provide feedback.

For drivers, the system will provide access to transportation opportunities, professional verification, reputation building, trip management and controlled access to their earnings.

For administrators, the system will provide the operational controls required to verify drivers, monitor trips, manage payments, resolve disputes and evaluate platform performance.

The modules defined in this chapter will form the foundation for the next stage of the CarGo Kenya system design, where the individual user workflows and system processes can be described in greater detail.
